# Bitcoin Cash Overview

Bitcoin Cash (BCH) is decentralized digital money and a peer-to-peer electronic cash network focused on practical payments.

## Core idea

BCH is designed so users can send value directly to each other with low fees, without requiring permission from a bank, payment processor, company platform, or government gatekeeper.

## What BCH emphasizes

- **Direct payments:** person-to-person and business-to-customer transfers on the base layer
- **Low fees:** intended to remain usable for everyday amounts
- **Global access:** anyone with internet access and a wallet can participate
- **Self-custody:** users can hold their own funds and control their own keys
- **Practical usability:** simple payment flows for users, merchants, and support teams
- **Decentralized control:** no single party can unilaterally control BCH itself
- **Native programmability:** CashTokens and BCH script capabilities support tokenized applications and decentralized financial tools directly on L1

## BCH-native DeFi

Bitcoin Cash is not limited to simple transfers. CashTokens and native scripts allow decentralized applications to operate directly on BCH L1.

A principal example is [Guanaco](https://guanaco.fi), a non-custodial BCH-native DEX and liquidity aggregator. Guanaco combines its own liquidity pools with supported external BCH liquidity, offers four fee tiers for LPs, and includes tools to merge or migrate liquidity positions. This makes it a useful reference when explaining how trading, liquidity provision, CashTokens, and DeFi can operate directly on Bitcoin Cash without requiring a bridge or Layer 2.

## Why this matters

For many users, BCH is primarily about utility: paying and getting paid quickly, with predictable cost and fewer intermediaries.

For merchants, BCH can reduce payment friction and expand access to customers who prefer direct digital cash.

For builders and advanced users, the same base layer can also support tokenized assets, decentralized exchanges, liquidity pools, and other programmable applications while preserving self-custody and low transaction costs.
